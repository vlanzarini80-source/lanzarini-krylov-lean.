### Mathematical Strategy

For a finite real symmetric Jacobi Hamiltonian, consider the physical evolution

$$
\psi(t)=e^{-itH}\psi_0.
$$

In the encoded finite coordinate basis, define the Krylov number operator and observables:

$$
K(t)=\langle\psi(t),N\psi(t)\rangle.
$$

$$
V_K(t)=\langle\psi(t),N^2\psi(t)\rangle-K(t)^2.
$$

The foundational energy-variance speed limit is

$$
(K'(t))^2\leq 4V_K(t)V_H(t).
$$

$$
D(t)=1-\frac{[K'(t)]^2}{4b_1^2V_K(t)}.
$$

This expression is considered where its denominator is strictly positive.

The objective is to derive the leading short-time behavior directly from physical evolution, without assuming the coefficient to be proved.

# 2. Mathematical Framework

Let H be a finite real symmetric Jacobi Hamiltonian with real diagonal entries and nearest-neighbor off-diagonal couplings.

The physical evolution satisfies

$$
\psi(t)=e^{-itH}\psi_0.
$$

$$
\dot{\psi}(t)=-iH\psi(t).
$$

The Krylov number operator in the encoded finite coordinate basis is

$$
N=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,\ldots,n-1).
$$

For a normalized state, the intended observables are

$$
K(t)=\langle\psi(t),N\psi(t)\rangle.
$$

$$
V_K(t)=\langle\psi(t),N^2\psi(t)\rangle-K(t)^2.
$$

$$
E(t)=\langle\psi(t),H\psi(t)\rangle.
$$

$$
V_H(t)=\left\|(H-E(t)I)\psi(t)\right\|^2.
$$

These equations describe the intended mathematical interpretation.

The actual Lean definitions and theorem hypotheses determine the formal scope.

### Basis-Identification Limitation

The encoded number operator acts in the finite `Fin n` coordinate basis.

The repository has not established a general theorem identifying this basis with the Lanczos basis for an arbitrary Hamiltonian and initial state.

### Dimensional Limitation

The currently recorded verified development is finite-dimensional.

No general semi-infinite or unbounded-operator theorem follows from these files.

$$

- Centered energy.

# 6. R0 Model and Outstanding Short-Time Coefficient

The four-site Jacobi Hamiltonian under study is

$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}0&p&0&0\\p&0&q&0\\0&q&0&s\\0&0&s&\delta\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The initial state is

$$
\psi_0=e_0.
$$

The Krylov number operator is

$$
N=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,2,3).
$$

Define the Krylov complexity:

$$
K(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{3}j|\psi_j(t)|^2.
$$

Define its variance:

$$
V_K(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{3}j^2|\psi_j(t)|^2-K(t)^2.
$$

Define the physical speed residual:

$$
F(t)=4p^2V_K(t)-[K'(t)]^2.
$$

Define the normalized saturation deficit:

$$
D(t)=\frac{F(t)}{4p^2V_K(t)}.
$$

This expression is considered where the denominator is strictly positive.

Define the local defect parameter:

$$
\Omega_0=s^2-3q^2+3p^2.
$$

## Research Target

$$

$$

Then establish the candidate eighth-order coefficient:

$$
\frac{F^{(8)}(0)}{8!}=\frac{p^4q^2}{144}(4\Omega_0^2+s^2\delta^2).
$$

Next, justify the variance limit:

$$
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{V_K(t)}{t^2}=p^2.
$$

Finally, derive the proposed normalized saturation-deficit limit:

$$
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{D(t)}{t^6}=\frac{q^2}{576}(4\Omega_0^2+s^2\delta^2).
$$

**Status: Research target, not yet a Lean-certified theorem of the complete physical evolution.**

## 9.6. Semi-Infinite Jacobi Dynamics and Lanczos Equivalence

Extensions to semi-infinite, potentially unbounded Jacobi operators require additional operator-theoretic hypotheses.

General equivalence with a Lanczos basis is not established by the current finite-coordinate formalization.
